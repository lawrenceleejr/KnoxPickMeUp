# Knox Pick-Me-Up — Funding Applications

The pilot's grant strategy in one line: **DKA funds the printed kit now, a
City seed covers launch, and a THSO application in March funds the scale-up**
— so no single funder carries the program, and each is asked for the thing
they exist to fund. Budget context: the 6-month pilot is ~$18,000 all-in
([PROGRAM.md §4](PROGRAM.md)); printing is $2,500–4,000 of it
([RFQ-PILOT.md](RFQ-PILOT.md)); coffee and rides are in-kind.

---

## 1. DKA Quality of Life grant — ready to submit

The Downtown Knoxville Alliance (the CBID's 501(c)(3)) makes Quality of Life
grants for projects that benefit the public inside the district. Their funded
categories include **public safety**, **elimination of problems related to
traffic and parking**, and **promotion and marketing** — three direct hits.

**The filled application:**
[`funding/DKA-Quality-of-Life-Application-KnoxPickMeUp.docx`](funding/DKA-Quality-of-Life-Application-KnoxPickMeUp.docx)
— the official form completed for a **$4,000 request** (the printed kit) of
the $18,000 total, with the required project budget (Attachment A) and
artwork renderings (Attachment B) already embedded, and the D1/H1 question
marked n/a (no permanent improvements; all materials are removable collateral
inside private venues).

Before sending, fill the three placeholders — `[Your name]`,
`[Your mailing address]`, `[Your phone]` — and the anticipated start/completion
dates, then email the file to **Michele Hummel, Downtown Knoxville Alliance —
mhummel@downtownknoxville.org** (questions: 865.246.2654).

Guidelines that shape the ask:

- **Grants are paid on completion, by reimbursement or direct billing** — so
  either front the print bill or, better, ask DKA to be **billed directly by
  the print shop** (name the shop in the award paperwork once the
  [RFQ](RFQ-PILOT.md) quote lands).
- **12 months to complete** — the 6-month pilot fits with room to spare.
- Applications are evaluated on criteria, budget, and best return to DKA —
  which is why the application leads with per-venue data reporting and member
  benefits, and why the DKA pitch email in [OUTREACH.md](OUTREACH.md) should
  land *before or with* the application, not after.

## 2. Tennessee Highway Safety Office — next window: March 2027

THSO administers NHTSA pass-through highway-safety grants (about $33.5M
statewide in the round announced August 2025). The relevant categories are
**Impaired Driving Education** ("community-based prevention programs") and
**Community Traffic Safety Programs** — Knox Pick-Me-Up's exact lane.

The mechanics, per THSO's published process:

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Application window | **March 1–31, annually** (closed for this year — next is **March 1–31, 2027**, funding the federal fiscal year starting October 2027) |
| Portal | [THSOgrants.org](https://thsogrants.org/Login2.aspx?APPTHEME=TNGHSO) |
| Eligible applicants | Local governments, law enforcement, academic institutions, **private nonprofits** |
| Required with the application | **Three years of baseline statistics** for the jurisdiction relevant to the targeted crash type, plus comparison against similar jurisdictions |
| Contact | info@tntrafficsafety.org · 1-800-99-BELTS |

**The eligibility catch:** an all-volunteer program isn't an eligible
applicant. Knox Pick-Me-Up needs an **applicant of record**, in order of fit:

1. **City of Knoxville** — applies routinely; folds into the city
   partnership ask already in [OUTREACH.md](OUTREACH.md);
2. **A 501(c)(3) partner** — DKA itself qualifies, as would another local
   nonprofit acting as fiscal agent;
3. **University of Tennessee** — an academic-institution application with an
   evaluation component (natural fit given the program's metrics design).

**Why the timing works, not hurts:** the pilot runs on DKA + City + sponsor
money starting now; by March 2027 the application carries the pilot's *own*
issuance/redemption/venue data on top of the regional baseline — exactly the
evidence THSO scoring rewards. The ask shifts from "fund an idea" to "fund
the scale-up of a measured program": coordinator, second-wave venue
expansion, evaluation, and reprints.

**Narrative skeleton** (drafted to reuse; sources already live in
[PROGRAM.md](PROGRAM.md) §1 and §9):

- *Problem identification:* ~67 serious injuries and 27 deaths per year in
  regional impaired-driving crashes (Knoxville Regional TPO); 1 in 3 Knox
  County crash deaths involves an impaired driver (Knox County Health Dept);
  three years of downtown-zone late-night crash/DUI data from KPD and state
  crash records — assemble this with the applicant of record.
- *Countermeasure justification:* alternative-transportation programs are a
  recognized NHTSA countermeasure (*Countermeasures That Work*), and the 2020
  *Journal of Safety Research* synthesis identifies the success attributes
  this program was designed around: low cost to the patron, high awareness,
  convenience, rides both directions, multiple sponsors.
- *Activities:* card issuance at partner bars tied to proof of a safe ride;
  morning redemption at partner shops; KAT free-fare window; venue and
  community signage; staff training.
- *Evaluation:* serialized scan-logged issuance/redemption per venue per
  night; downtown late-night crash and DUI trends with KPD (small-sample
  caveats stated); quarterly public reporting.
- *Budget shape:* coordinator (part-time), materials/reprints, staff
  training, evaluation — modest and mostly personnel, which THSO categories
  accommodate.
